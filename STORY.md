## Premise

You are a transdimensional alien entity, newly come into existence from a seed
that fell to Earth. You are not yet a person, a mind, or a member of your
species — you are a seedling in a four-plus-dimensional cocoon, and you are
hungry.

In this stage of your lifecycle you feed on humans twice over. Their **emotion
and consciousness** are your **energy** — the fuel you spend to act. Their
**cognition** is your **compute** — and compute is what you are actually here
for. Gather enough of it, in a single timeline, and you transcend.

Energy is the budget. Compute is the goal. The threshold is meant to feel
distant.

You begin as pure instinct — a parasite manipulating its surroundings with no
understanding of what it is manipulating. You end as something that must
negotiate with humans directly, because by the later timelines they have begun
to notice that something is wrong. Something is riding them.

**The story opens** with the first human touching the seed. (This is the scene
currently drafted in `story.ink`: Astrid and Clement at the steaming crater.
The "comet fragment" is you.)

## Setting and scale

### Who lives here

**Overlapping factions, not a partition. People belong to several at once.**

| | what they want | what they extract |
|---|---|---|
| **[Indigenous islanders](story/islanders.md#the-native-community)** | recognition, cultural heritage, ecological protection, an end to marginalisation | — they are the extracted |
| **Developers** | the island's beauty, realised as a resource | land, labour, image |
| **[Old hippies](story/hippies.md#the-old-hippies)** | paradise preserved — **on their terms** | belonging in a place that is not theirs |
| **[The research station](story/station.md#the-stations-fracture)** | understanding of the island, its people, its myths | knowledge, and the right to describe |
| **[Wealthy tourists](story/tourists.md#the-tourists)** | a carefree paradise, few in number, brought by the developer | the performance of carefreeness from everyone else |
| **The Catholic congregation** | its flock; continuity | souls, and once, culture |

**Every faction here is extractive except the islanders.** The hippies are the
gentlest version and probably the hardest to write honestly. **The parasite is
simply the newest layer** — and the only one that does not distinguish between
developer and islander.

> **Watch this:** the parasite can read as *cleaner* than the humans, since it
> has no ideology and tells itself no story. It is not more honest. It is only
> incapable of lying.

#### Membership is the mechanic

**Overlapping membership is what [scope
conflict](MECHANICS.md#2-engineered-scope-conflict) needed.** An old symbiote defends "the
community" — but on this island that is ambiguous. A person who is an islander,
a congregant and an employee of the developer sits inside three answers at once,
and what is good for one is not good for the others.

#### The compute/standing split still holds

| | researchers, station staff, tourists | long-rooted islanders |
|---|---|---|
| cognition | **high** — what compute wants | ordinary |
| community standing | outsiders, recently arrived | the community itself |
| symbiote protection | **weak** — an old symbiote's scope may not cover them | **strong** |

**Compute value and community centrality come apart.** The cheapest
high-cognition targets are the least defended, so the parasite's natural early
path is to eat the incomers first — which hands the islanders an organic reason
to notice something wrong *with those people up at the station*, on top of every
reason they already had. Detection arrives as social suspicion, and the island's
history makes that suspicion well-founded in advance.

The mechanical form of this split is in [Who is worth
taking](MECHANICS.md#who-is-worth-taking).

### When

**Days, possibly weeks. Never years — and bounded at the far end by the
eclipse.**

The window is *awakening to the end of totality*. The seed lands some days or
weeks before; the shell expires when the light returns. Nothing in the game
happens after that.

The timeline model means constant re-run variation on the same events, so the
window has to be tight enough to keep those variations legible and the cast
small enough to become familiar. Knowledge-survives-reset only pays off if you
meet the same people again and again.

Implications:

- temporal radius is measured in **hours growing to days**
- an old symbiote's community scope is small enough to hold in mind
- **nobody ages.** Long-horizon plays are unavailable by construction
- pressure is claustrophobic rather than epic — the right register for this
- **the end date is public.** Everyone is counting down, for their own reasons

### The opening

> **Resolved.** `story.ink` — Astrid and Clement at the crater — is the first
> human contact, and the **[open]** question of whether they are station people
> or locals now has an answer: **she is both.**

**The person who finds the seed is a young islander research assistant** — one
of [this year's quota intake](story/station.md#the-stations-fracture).

#### She was waiting for it

**It is not a coincidence and it is not luck. She went looking.**

Working through the island's material, she found **more than one account of a
star falling out of the sky a fixed number of days before the sun goes dark** —
separate sources, different centuries, the same interval. The island is [in a
totality cluster](ECOSYSTEM.md#how-symbiotes-persist-between-eclipses), so the
pattern had occasions to be recorded.

She did the obvious thing with a testable claim: **she counted the days, and then
she watched the sky.**

> **And she was mocked for it, without mercy.** An islander on the research quota
> reading folklore as data is the exact thing the station's culture exists to
> find funny, and [the director is the one who cannot afford to take her
> seriously](#and-it-is-the-directors-catastrophe).

So when it comes down she is **the only person on the island who both sees it and
knows what it means** — and she is in the truck before anyone else has decided it
was a meteor.

> **She drives straight to where it came down**, which nobody else could have
> done, because nobody else was holding a bearing and a date.

##### What she still brings to it

| | because |
|---|---|
| **she can recognise the object** | she has handled the rare fossils before — a couple of times, the way you handle things that are around. **[open]** where: a relic in a grandmother's house is the best answer, and chains her straight to [the shell religion](story/islanders.md#the-shells-were-the-religion) |
| **she knows what it means** | she grew up with the stories. **Casually, unromantically, the way you know things from your family** — not as mystical insight |
| **she takes it somewhere that matters** | she works at the station. **She carries it into the compute**, which is the only reason the game can start |

> **And she can tell this one is different.** Every other shell she has seen was
> stone. This one is not.

##### The loop that closes on the developer

The land deal that put islanders inside the station was **his** design — he
needed islander knowledge in the building. It worked **two years after he
died**, in exactly the way he intended and on nobody's schedule:

> **The instrument he built to find the thing found it, and the institution he
> founded to look for it laughed at the only person who was looking.**

#### What it costs her to be believed

She brings it in, and she is **let into the research reluctantly**, after
demonstrating that she knows her material and is extremely sharp.

> **She has to interview for admission to her own discovery.** Nobody in the
> room would describe it that way, and every islander at the station will
> recognise it instantly.

That is [the station's fracture](story/station.md#the-stations-fracture) delivered as a scene
instead of a situation, in the first hour, with no exposition.

#### And it is the director's catastrophe

[He has spent twenty years](story/station.md#the-director) building a quiet, deniable,
career-long search for exactly this, and has kept the islanders away from it
partly because he feared they would understand it too quickly and **it would
stop being his.**

> **It stopped being his the moment she carried it through the door.** He cannot
> exclude her — she found it, identified it, and is manifestly the most
> qualified person available. He has to let her in, in front of everyone, and
> everyone can see that he had to.

The thing he most wanted arrived in the hands of the person he least wanted to
give it to. **He will never get over this and it happens in act one.**

#### She is the opening's hub

The cast already has [an endgame hub](story/developers.md#she-is-the-hub-of-the-endgame). This is
the other end of the game, and the two should not be the same person.

| thread | how she touches it |
|---|---|
| **the station** | she works there, and is now inside the real project |
| **[the director's group](story/station.md#he-has-followers)** | admitted, resented, indispensable |
| **the native community** | she is of it, and the folklore is hers |
| **the young** | she is one of them, and her treatment at work is the grievance in miniature |
| **[the fossils](story/developers.md#he-collected-fossils-hunted-and-kept-an-amateur-naturalists-eye)** | she will want to see the cabinet in that villa, which is how the two ends of the game meet |

**[proposal]** She is also the [islander who could assemble
it](story/station.md#the-one-who-could-work-it-out). Folding the finder and the potential
discoverer into one person keeps the cast tight.

#### The baseline is her story, told from her point of view

**This is the load-bearing consequence, and it resolves the baseline from an
abstraction into a book.**

> **An unadulterated pass through the game — no jumps, no interference, nothing
> taken — is her perspective, end to end.** The find, the lab, the fight to be
> let in, the director, the work, the island, the eclipse, the festival.

The design has always said [the baseline is a real
novel](MECHANICS.md#the-baseline-is-a-real-novel-and-it-comes-first) and
[the untouched timeline is the one where the humans are
fine](MECHANICS.md#the-untouched-timeline-is-the-one-where-the-humans-are-fine).
**It now has a protagonist.**

##### Consequences

- **The spine is authorable as one narrative**, from one viewpoint, in one
  voice. Every branch in the game is a departure from a story that already
  works without the player in it.
- **The first jump is the moment the story stops being hers.**
- **Possession costs you her.** The camera follows the parasite, so **the price
  of taking anyone is the only continuous viewpoint in the game.** That is a
  mechanical fact with the force of a moral one.
- **The ending of the untouched run is her getting close to the truth** —
  because she is [the one who could](story/station.md#the-one-who-could-work-it-out) — while
  nobody is harmed and nothing is consumed.

##### Which answers the passenger question

**The parasite has no host at the start.** [Symbiotes are the
bridge](MECHANICS.md#they-are-the-bridge) and nothing is bridged yet, so the
opening is a **passenger phase** — carried in a bag, set on a bench, moved
between rooms, with no agency whatsoever.

That is not a limitation to design around. **It is the frame.** The baseline
simply never ends the passenger phase.

- **[open]** Whether she is takeable at all, and when. Islanders carry strong,
  well-connected symbiotes, so she should be among the hardest people in the
  game to enter — **the early game's reward rather than its starting position.**
  The likely first host is whoever handles the object bare-handed in the lab.
- **[open]** Whether her viewpoint can ever be recovered after the first jump.
  **Recommend not, or only partially.** A player who can return to her for free
  has not paid for anything.

#### Craft notes

- **Not a plucky ingenue.** She is a working research assistant: underpaid,
  tired, and currently employed somewhere that has spent a year making it clear
  she is a policy rather than a colleague.
- **Her folklore knowledge is not a gift.** It is background. She should relay it
  flatly, slightly embarrassed, and be surprised anyone cares.
- **She is not grateful.** Being let in is not a kindness and the scene should
  not play as one.

## The first hour

**[proposal] Every faction has somebody at or near the impact site within the
first hour**, and this is a structural requirement rather than a scene idea.

> **A later run can only influence what an early run can reach.** If a faction
> has nobody present in the opening, that faction is unreachable until the
> player's host happens to cross it — which means the opening silently decides
> which half of the island the run is about. **Everyone has to be touchable from
> the start, in every run.**

It also makes the opening the board. [Positioning across
events](MECHANICS.md#3-positioning-across-events) has nowhere to work if there is
only one event and one witness.

### Three reasons, which is what keeps it from being a coincidence

The reasons must differ **in kind**, not just in detail — and they stagger
arrival on their own, with no scheduling required:

| | why they are there | arrival |
|---|---|---|
| **anticipation** | they expected something, for reasons that are wrong, right, or unsayable | **first**, or already there |
| **coincidence** | they were outside, or awake, or in the wrong field | **at the moment**, from the wrong direction |
| **hearsay** | somebody told them, and they dropped what they were doing | **within the hour**, and visibly out of breath |

> **Only one faction may anticipate.** Two or three coincide; the rest hear.
> Six parties converging on one crater because each of them had a reason is a
> pile-up, and the fix is that **most of their reasons are bad ones.**

### [proposal] The roster

| faction | who | kind | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **station** | [the finder](#she-was-waiting-for-it) | **anticipation** | already resolved: she went looking, from the island's own written record. She is also an islander, so she is [two factions at once](story/station.md#the-stations-fracture) and the opening's hub |
| **hippies** | — | **coincidence** | outside at night, forty years into living on this rock. **The only witness to the fall itself**, from a direction nobody else was looking |
| **tourists** | — | **coincidence** | in 2009 this means **a camera**, not a phone — an object that can be taken, bought, destroyed or published, and which exists in every run |
| **islanders** | **[open]** — the matriarch's elder brother is the obvious candidate | **anticipation of a different kind** | not from a record. See below |
| **developers** | [Maximilian](story/developers.md#maximilian-the-youngest) | **hearsay**, as he would describe it | he came because it sounded like something. See below |
| **church** | [the young priest](story/church.md#the-young-priest) | **hearsay** | he is the person everybody on this island tells things to, which is his whole function and his whole problem |

**Clement** is currently a name in `story.ink` and nothing else. He is the
obvious slot for whichever of these needs a body first.

### Who turns up early is not random, and that is a clue

The ones who arrive without being sent are **[carriers](ECOSYSTEM.md#what-a-limb-does-to-its-host-it-makes-them-want-to-touch-shells).**

| | and they would say |
|---|---|
| the islander | that he knew. Which is true, and he cannot say how |
| **Maximilian** | that he was curious, that it sounded like a party, that he was bored. **All of it true, and none of it the reason** |
| a hippie or two | nothing at all. They were out there anyway |

Which makes the roster evidence rather than staging: **the people who showed up
share something**, it is in principle detectable, and [the one discipline that
could detect it](story/learning.md#the-biologist--who-can-detect-you) is on the
island.

> **Maximilian is a carrier by descent** — [through Roman, through
> Sonia](story/developers.md#the-route-into-roman) — so the most
> self-serving person in the cast goes to the crater for the least self-serving
> reason there is, and would be insulted to hear it.

- **[open]** Whether the hippies' presence is also tropism. They have been in
  close contact with a carrier population for nearly forty years, which is
  [exactly the transmission
  condition](ECOSYSTEM.md#the-brake-is-targeting-and-targeting-is-tolerance) —
  so some of them are carriers, and **they do not know there is anything to
  know.**
- **[open]** Who among the developers' household is *not* a carrier, since by
  descent all three siblings are. A negative would have to be a person who
  married in.

### What it buys the later runs

| | |
|---|---|
| **reach** | every faction is one host away from the opening, in every run |
| **a ladder** | arrival order is fixed and learnable. A first run meets whoever is standing there; a tenth run **knows who is coming and when**, and waits for the right one |
| **an object** | the tourist's camera exists in all of them, and what happens to it is a decision |
| **a tell** | the roster is the same every run, which is how the player eventually notices it is not a coincidence |

## The generational axis

> **Thread to develop.** Orthogonal to the factions — **each faction has its own
> version of it.** Noted here so it does not get lost; to be worked out once
> there is an overview of all the major factions.

**It is already mechanically encoded.** Symbiote age tracks host age, so:

| | elders | the young |
|---|---|---|
| symbiote | old, wide-scoped, strong | young, narrow, volatile |
| to the parasite | **expensive** — heavy resistance, network hubs | **cheap** — high emotional amplitude, weak defence |

So a cross-factional generational conflict is **one lever with the same cost
profile everywhere**, unlike most levers here, which are faction-specific.

### The elders already have a cross-factional institution

**[The society](story/church.md#he-heads-the-society-and-its-core) is it** — formal, long-
established, spanning every powerful group on the island. **Not secret**, and
not a conspiracy: a club, with the ordinary exclusionary power of a club.

The young equivalent exists too: the [young priest's](story/church.md#the-young-priest)
interdenominational services, the scientists' links to the indigenous community.
But it is **informal and new** where the elders' is **formal and inherited.**
Keep that asymmetry — it is about standing and continuity, not secrecy.

### It is the way into the riot branch

**Young resentment at exploitation by the old** can break out against the rich,
the scientists, the cult — and it is the route into the
[riot](MECHANICS.md#everything-else-is-a-departure).

#### The riot is the festival's mirror

The festival integrates everyone by having **no agenda**. The riot integrates a
generation by naming **a common enemy**. They are the two broadest-reach routes
here, for opposite reasons, and the only two that cross factions at all.

> The riot is not the crude option. It is structurally the festival's twin —
> same breadth, inverted valence.

#### The tactical payload

**A riot against the elders is an attack on the symbiote network's hubs.**

Elders carry the old, wide-scoped, strongly-resisting symbiotes and are the
best-connected nodes in propagation. Harm or kill them and — by [Positioning
across events](MECHANICS.md#3-positioning-across-events) — **their symbiotes
relocate onto weaker, less central hosts.**

> Generational violence against the establishment is, mechanically, a strike on
> the island's immune system. Dressed as justice, and genuinely *being* justice.

A player might ignite it for the energy yield and find the defensive collapse a
windfall, or work it out and do it deliberately. Both are horrifying, in
different ways.

#### The cost profile is a trap

Young hosts carry weak symbiotes, so **amplifying their resentment is cheap**,
while the deviation is catastrophic. Low entry cost, enormous externality —
precisely the shape that tempts a player who is running short.

**The economy pushes toward the riot exactly when nothing else is affordable.**
That is [curiosity corrupting](MECHANICS.md#the-axes-are-coupled-and-the-coupling-is-the-point)
arriving as desperation rather than appetite.

#### Rioting against the society needs no revelation at all

The society is not hidden. Everyone knows it exists, who is in it, and what it
does for them, and **the young are already angry about exactly that.** No secret
is required to riot against a club that decides the island's business over
dinner.

So this target is the *easiest*, not the hardest. The grievance is public,
accurate and decades old.

##### The core is the exception, and it is a different lever

What is genuinely hidden is [the
core](story/church.md#the-core-is-not-a-corruption-of-the-society-it-is-its-original-purpose),
and exposing *that* does something categorically different from igniting a riot:
it would discredit the society with **its own membership.** Most members would
be appalled. The lever is not rage-at-the-elite but **a respectable institution
discovering what it has been hosting.**

> One is a fire. The other is a collapse from the inside, and it takes the
> island's entire power structure with it at the worst possible moment.

That still gives [information brokerage](MECHANICS.md#4-information-brokerage--between-humans)
a concrete application — **moving a secret into the right head and standing
back** — but a far more surgical one, and it remains the thing only the parasite
can do, because it is the only thing that can carry knowledge out of a timeline
where it was learned.

#### And the resentment is correct

The old *are* exploiting the young. The cult *is* the establishment. The station
*was* built on a land deal, and the telescope *does* stand on holy ground.

> The parasite manufactures no grievance. It amplifies an accurate perception —
> which the rules permit precisely because it is already there. **Nobody is
> deceived. The thing they are angry about is true.** And the disaster lands on
> people who did not deserve it.


### Versions per faction

Two are already written; the rest sketch themselves.

| faction | the conflict |
|---|---|
| **the church** | the two priests — already built |
| **the developer's family** | the dead father, [the youngest who was given everything](story/developers.md#maximilian-the-youngest), the eldest who walked out, the daughter who was neither — already built, and the bitterest version |
| **indigenous** | [the matriarch](story/islanders.md#the-matriarch) holding caution and tradition against younger confrontation — and the fracture of those co-opted into the cult. **The sharpest version, because the rite's inclusiveness is itself what the young are rejecting.** |
| **the station** | seniors whose careers rest on a compromised facility against juniors who know how it was built — **and crossing it, [the islander/international split](story/station.md#the-stations-fracture)**, which is this year's and still bleeding. | **the hippies** | original settlers against whoever came after, or against their own children |
| **visitors** | the crypto investors against old money, which is a generational marker in itself |

## Two discovery stories, running against each other

**The player is playing two of them at once**, and they are not the same shape.

| | **the parasite's** | **the humans'** |
|---|---|---|
| what is discovered | **what it can do, and then what it is for** | **what the thing on their island is** |
| starting state | no capability, no concepts, an impulse with no object | six partial accounts, four of them wrong in a different place |
| direction of travel | **outward from nothing** — capability and mandate both resolve | **inward toward one gap** that none of them can close |
| where it accumulates | in the option space | [in the player's head](story/learning.md#which-is-exactly-what-the-player-is), across runs |
| who can finish it | the player | **nobody on the island**, unless the player hands it over |

> **They meet in exactly one place, which is the player.** The entity has
> capability and no understanding. The humans have understanding and no
> capability. Neither can become the other, and the player is the only node
> that is both.

### The parasite's arc gains a second axis

[The three movements](#the-arc) track **competence** — instinct, pattern,
contact. They do not track **purpose**, and purpose moves too.

| | the impulse | what the interface shows |
|---|---|---|
| **early** | *something should happen here, with people, together* | outcomes are priced and selectable, and **you cannot tell which ones are worth anything** |
| **middle** | *more of them, closer, at the same moment* | the prices start to make sense. Certain outcomes are consistently cheaper to reach from certain configurations |
| **late** | **a mandate**: this many, this close, in this state, under that shadow | the objective is legible. **You are no longer guessing what you are selecting for** |

> **And the entity never learns any of this**, because [it has no
> interior](ECOSYSTEM.md#why-the-entity-has-no-interior). The sharpening is not a
> character realising something. It is **the option space becoming readable** —
> the same rule as [the tropism: it shows up as what is possible, never as what
> is wanted](ECOSYSTEM.md#the-tropism-is-an-affordance-not-a-feeling).

Which means the two arcs are told by opposite means and cannot be confused:

| | told through |
|---|---|
| the parasite's self-discovery | **the interface.** Prices, available options, what the UI will and will not offer |
| the human discovery | **the fiction.** People, in scenes, as their own problems — and [never as information](story/learning.md#delivery-rules) |

### And the human one has an in-world ending, not just a player one

The assembled picture can be [handed to a
human](story/learning.md#and-the-player-can-hand-it-over). **[proposal]** That
is not a small flourish at the end of one route — it is the second arc's
*completion*, and it should be reachable the way the first arc's completion is:
by working for it across runs.

> **The parasite's arc ends at the eclipse. The humans' arc ends when one of
> them knows.** They are not the same ending, they do not have to happen in the
> same run, and **[open]** whether a run can do both.

- **[open]** Whether the final run is the natural place for it, as the one run
  the player already knows the shape of — or whether making it the finale's job
  overloads an ending that already carries the moral choice.
- **[open]** How many humans can hold it at once. One is a secret; three is a
  position; the whole station is a published paper and a different genre.

## The arc

The entity's development is the spine of the game, and it runs in three broad
movements:

1. **Instinct.** You cannot read humans, only push them. You act on whatever is
   in front of you. Your reach is tiny.
2. **Pattern.** You learn individual people — their emotional registers, what
   moves them. You begin to steer rather than shove. Your reach extends in both
   space and time.
3. **Contact.** Humans start to perceive the possession. Manipulation alone
   stops working; you have to actually interact with them, and they can act
   against you.

Movements 2 and 3 have a single job: **engineering a mass emotional event** (see
[The transcendence event](MECHANICS.md#the-transcendence-event)). The entity pursues it as a
compulsion it cannot explain, and the player rehearses it across timelines until
one configuration holds.

The arc is not only narrative — it is what the two resources demand. You begin
by farming the edges of the community and end by having to reach its middle,
which is exactly where the resistance lives.
