## Reference points

### Blindsight, Peter Watts (2006)

**The closest ancestor of the premise, on two counts at once.**

| | Watts | here |
|---|---|---|
| **intelligence without consciousness** | the scramblers — hugely capable, modelling and acting with no inner experience; consciousness treated as a separable and possibly maladaptive accident | the parasite has [no interior at all](../ECOSYSTEM.md#the-player-supplies-the-interior), faculties rather than concepts, and borrows every thought it has |
| **a folk myth given an evolutionary account** | vampires as a real extinct human predator, the aversion to crosses explained as a seizure response to right angles | **possession, guardian spirits and tricksters** as [garbled observation of a real species](islanders.md#their-myths-are-the-most-accurate-account-on-the-island) |

The design's note that human consciousness may itself be *a model the brain runs
rather than a substance it has* sits on the same ground Watts builds on.

#### Where this has to differ

- **Watts puts the non-conscious intelligence outside and looks at it. This puts
  the player inside one** — and then [refuses to furnish
  it](../ECOSYSTEM.md#the-player-supplies-the-interior). Blindsight narrates a human
  with impaired empathy and observes the scramblers; here the viewpoint slot is
  empty and the player fills it.
- **Watts is arguing a thesis** — that consciousness is a costly evolutionary
  accident. **This design argues nothing.** The entity simply lacks one, and
  [the game never takes a position on
  it](../ECOSYSTEM.md#what-kind-of-being-this-is). Keeping it a property rather
  than a claim is the difference between a reference and a derivative.
- **Interiority is not a property of the entity at all.** Watts's scramblers lack
  it as a biological fact he argues for; here the entity simply has none and the
  text never supplies one. What the player reads is always **a host's** inner
  life.
- **The myth treatment differs in direction.** Watts explains a myth away. Here
  the folklore is **accurate and badly transmitted** — the interest is in what
  survives a thousand years of retelling, not in debunking.

### Tenet, Christopher Nolan (2020)

**The closest ancestor of the temporal mechanic**, and on the same physics rather
than a parallel invention.

| | Nolan | here |
|---|---|---|
| **"what's happened, happened"** | the **Novikov self-consistency principle** — one timeline, paradox impossible because only self-consistent histories exist | the same formalism: [post-selected CTCs](../ECOSYSTEM.md#frameworks-to-lean-on-none-of-them-invented-here), where **post-selection is the fold** |
| **the temporal pincer** | two teams, one running forward from the setup, one backward from the outcome, the operation happening where both constrain it | [the two-state vector](../ECOSYSTEM.md#across-timelines) — a state fixed by boundary conditions at both ends of the run |
| **nobody changes anything** | the protagonist discovers he was always the one who did it | the shell does not intervene. [It was always going to have](../MECHANICS.md#the-option-space) |

The pincer is worth keeping in mind as the one staged, watchable version of a
two-boundary system. It is what TSVF looks like when someone films it.

#### What to take

**Consequences legible, mechanism opaque.** Nobody in the film explains inversion
usefully and everybody can see what it does. That is a working demonstration at
scale of the rule [the physics section is written
under](../ECOSYSTEM.md#the-mechanism): the machinery exists so the rules are
consistent, not so anyone explains them.

#### Where this has to differ

- **Nolan has nowhere to put the interiority.** A protagonist with almost none,
  inside a mechanism elaborate enough to crowd out everything else. This design
  relocates it rather than omitting it: the entity has none, **the hosts have all
  of it**, and [the baseline is a real
  novel](../MECHANICS.md#the-baseline-is-a-real-novel-and-it-comes-first) before it
  is anything else.
- **The Algorithm is the structural risk.** An abstract object accumulated so the
  ending can occur. [The purified
  resource](../MECHANICS.md#the-resource-buys-the-finale-it-is-not-the-finale) is
  the same shape, and the protection is that it never carries weight on its own —
  what it buys is **a choice about people**, not an object.
- **Inversion is not what the shell does.** Nolan's inverted matter runs
  thermodynamics backwards. Nothing here is entropy-reversed; the shell simply
  has [boundary conditions at both
  ends](../ECOSYSTEM.md#across-timelines). Borrow the image, not the mechanism.

- **Zero Escape: Virtue's Last Reward** — a navigable DAG of timelines as the
  primary UI. The closest existing precedent for the timeline mechanic.
- **Elsinore** — inhabit and observe, knowledge persists across loops, NPCs run
  on independent schedules.
- **The Forgotten City**, **Outer Wilds** — knowledge as the only real
  progression.
- **Fallen London** — storylets at scale (see `ARCHITECTURE.md`).

### For the register

The design's machinery — possession, escalating non-consensual influence, a cult,
a ritual fixed to an astronomical date — is the machinery of exploitation horror.
These are the works that use the same parts seriously:

- **The Wicker Man** (1973) — a remote community, an outsider, a fixed ritual
  date, a cosmology with its own internal logic. The direct structural ancestor.
- **Midsommar** — collective ecstatic ritual including sexual rite, filmed
  anthropologically rather than for titillation. The clearest demonstration that
  the difference is authorial stance, not content.
- **Under the Skin** — a non-human predator on humans, borrowed and incomplete
  interiority, never explained. The closest reference for the POV problem.
- **Picnic at Hanging Rock** — awe, disappearance, a refusal to account for
  itself.
- **Annihilation** — biology that does not care about you and is not malicious.
- **"Goliath"**, Neil Gaiman (1999) — an official Matrix tie-in using
  humans-as-computation rather than batteries. The closest existing execution of
  this premise. *(Note: the widespread claim that this was the Matrix's original
  script concept appears apocryphal — the earliest known drafts still have
  batteries. Gaiman's story is probably the source of the belief.)*

> **The distinction worth holding onto:** in the Matrix, either version, humans
> are unconscious, podded and fully simulated — the horror is imprisonment. Here
> nobody is imprisoned. People are awake, free, living their own lives, and
> something borrows their spare capacity while they do. **That nightmare
> requires a prison; this one requires only a Tuesday.**
