# The church

The old priest and the young priest — the clearest version of the generational axis.

> **Raw character material.** Not yet reconciled with each other or with the
> faction structure. Mechanical consequences noted where they are already clear.
>
> **Everything here is subject to [the standing rule on
> characters](../ECOSYSTEM.md#nobody-is-a-caricature-and-nobody-is-only-an-archetype):**
> each of these people is doing the best they believe they can achieve in a
> difficult situation, their one-line handle is a navigation aid rather than a

## The old priest

Fire and brimstone. He preaches the devil, demons, temptation, Sodom and
Gomorrah — **demons that drive people to unspeakable deeds *en masse***,
cryptically referencing the island's past.

He calls the eclipse a sign of God turning away, the time of the devil, and
chastises the scientists for masking the fear and terrible awe of such an event
with their silly explanations. He hates the science community.

### He rarely says mass because he was eased out, not because he cannot

He is entirely capable. **The diocese pushed him aside** — gently, over years, without
anything being said plainly — after **complaints about his teaching.** It does
not align with doctrine, and it is far too robust for modern sensibilities.
Parishioners complained. The complaints were reasonable.

**That is why the [young priest](#the-young-priest) is here.** A cardinal with
responsibility for the diocese brought him in, and everyone involved understands
what that means, and nobody has ever said it out loud.

> **He was not retired for being wrong about the world. He was retired for being
> impossible in a parish**, which is a different thing and a far more ordinary
> one. No conspiracy removed him. A complaints process did.

#### The irony is exact

What the diocese hears as eccentric, doctrinally loose, embarrassingly vivid
preaching **is [the core's](#the-core-is-not-a-corruption-of-the-society-it-is-its-original-purpose)
content coming through the pulpit.** He never stopped saying it. He was simply
moved somewhere it could not be heard.

> **He was sidelined for the one thing about him that happens to be true** —
> and the institution that did it was acting reasonably on every piece of
> information it had.

#### He still has one room where he is listened to properly

He gives the society's gatherings **no-holds-barred services**: everything the
parish complained about, undiluted, to people who want it.

And this is the texture that keeps the whole thing human — **most of them are
not believers.** For the not-so-devout membership the old man's services are
**part of the thrill of belonging.** They have the taste of something forbidden.
You come away a little rattled and you tell nobody, and that is exactly the
point.

| who is in that room | why they are there |
|---|---|
| the core | they mean every word |
| most of the membership | **transgression, and the pleasure of being inside something** |
| the old priest | **the only place left where he is taken seriously** |

> So the boundary between the society and its core is **not a wall. It is a
> gradient of how seriously you take it** — which is how these things actually
> work, and it removes the last of the horror-trope framing.

#### Three consequences

- **The core's rite has an audience**, which matters for the arithmetic. It is
  not six men in a cellar; it is six men and everyone who came for the frisson.
  Headcount is higher than a cult gathering should be, the intensity is genuine
  because transgression genuinely arouses, and **nobody present was coerced into
  anything.**
- **Thrill converts to belief under pressure, with no help from you.** As the
  island tightens toward totality, some fraction of the people who came for the
  taste of it will stop treating it as a taste. That escalation is an entirely
  human mechanism — the parasite can only accelerate what the eclipse was going
  to do anyway.
- **He is a man who gave his life to an institution that has quietly told him he
  is an embarrassment**, and who has one room left. Per [the standing rule on
  characters](../ECOSYSTEM.md#nobody-is-a-caricature-and-nobody-is-only-an-archetype),
  that is the whole of his motivation and it is sufficient.

### He heads the society, and its core

> Secret societies, lodges, sects and orders are **ordinary**, especially in
> rural places. They concentrate power and therefore raise the risk of abuse of the
> powerless — but so do chambers of commerce, parish councils and golf clubs.
> **For most people on this island, the local order is not meaningfully
> different from a country club.** Write it that way.

**"The cult" is two things at two radii.**

| | **the society** (outer) | **the core** (inner) |
|---|---|---|
| who | most of the island's elders, across every powerful faction | the old priest and a handful of serious members |
| what it is | a social institution — dues, dinners, charity drives, bad speeches, introductions, contracts, a seat at a table | genuinely occult practice, pursued in earnest |
| how secret | **not really.** Everyone knows it exists and roughly who is in it. The content of meetings is private the way a board's is private. | **actually hidden**, including from the society around it |
| what it does to outsiders | the ordinary exclusion of any elite club: you are not in the room where things are decided | nothing visible at all |
| how it reads | dull | embarrassing, to the outer members, if they saw it — *old Father X takes it rather literally* |

#### The core is not a corruption of the society. It is its original purpose.

The society was founded for what the core still does. The body around it **drifted into being a lodge** — kept the form, the
calendar and the standing, and quietly let the content go.

> **The sincere ones are the authentic ones.** The ordinary membership is the
> deviation. That inverts the usual shape of this trope, where a respectable
> institution turns out to have a dark secret: here a serious institution turned
> out to have a respectable majority.

Christian in shape, occult in practice: they stave off the reign of Satan by
**placating him** with old rites. The ordinary member hears that as inherited
language and a slightly odd annual observance. The core means it, and the
tension ramps as totality approaches. There are references to **a human
sacrifice in the island's dark past** — which the society treats as local
folklore and the core does not.

#### Three mechanical jobs, now assigned to the right radius

- **The society is a network and a venue.** It is made of elders, which means
  the oldest, widest-scoped symbiotes and the best-connected nodes in
  propagation — all of them in one room on a schedule. Valuable to the parasite
  as a contact graph long before it is interesting as a conspiracy.
- **The core is a gathering with a protocol**, escalating toward totality: a
  ready-made [transcendence event](../MECHANICS.md#the-transcendence-event)
  configuration, narrow and self-selected, and the natural vehicle for the
  darkest route.
- **The past sacrifice is a previous eclipse.** The core's practices are garbled
  operational memory — placating a devil that was a parasite, with rites that may
  once have worked for reasons nobody retained. **The core is therefore the only
  group of humans on the island actively preparing for what is actually coming**,
  for entirely wrong reasons, and the most likely to recognise it when it
  arrives.

#### The exclusion is real, and it is not special

The matriarch is outside this power structure and it weakens her. **So is
everyone else who is outside it** — the young, the station's juniors, the
incomers, the poor, most of the island. That is what a concentration of power
does, and it needs no occult explanation to be a genuine political fact.

> Do not let the society carry moral weight it has not earned. It is a
> mechanism of exclusion of exactly the ordinary kind. **The design's critique
> of it is the same critique it makes of the development, the station and the
> hotel — and it does not need to be a devil-worship story to land.**

#### It is doing the same thing the islanders are doing

**This is the key to writing it without caricature.** The society is a group of
people **trying to preserve an old identity in a present that has no use for
it** — which is precisely [the native community's
project](islanders.md#the-native-community), described in the same words.

| | **the community** | **the society** |
|---|---|---|
| the project | keep the tradition alive, adapted to the 21st century | keep the order alive, adapted to the 21st century |
| what it descends from | a real encounter with the entity at past eclipses | **the same encounter** |
| what it kept | the **practice** — intact, communal, performed in full | the **standing** — the calendar, the form, the seat |
| what it let go | the power, which it never had | the content, which survives only in the core |
| the young | find harmonisation an inadequate answer to injustice | find the whole thing an embarrassment and a racket |
| position | **outside** the power structure | **is** the power structure |

> **Same project, opposite ends of the island.** One preserves an identity with
> nothing behind it but the effort of the people doing it; the other preserves
> one backed by property, contracts and standing. **That difference is the
> entire moral distance between them — and it is a difference of position, not
> of sincerity.**

And they are **mirrored decay patterns of one original event.** Both traditions
began in a real encounter with something that arrived at totality. The community
kept the ceremony and lost nothing of it. The society kept the institution and
lost the ceremony to a handful of men who still mean it.

#### Which makes the old priest and the matriarch counterparts

They are in **structurally identical positions**: an elder holding a
preservation project together, arguing its worth to a younger generation that
has stopped finding it convincing, with a personal compromise in their past and
a deadline coming.

> They would understand each other immediately and will almost certainly never
> say so. **[proposal]** One scene of mutual recognition between them, late,
> brief, and resolving nothing, is worth more than any amount of explanation of
> either institution.

It also humanises him substantially without softening the critique by a single
degree: he is still at the centre of an exclusionary power structure, and he is
still doing the thing the game treats sympathetically when she does it.

### He has been suppressing his own symbiote for fifty years

**[strong]** He is partly aware of it. He reads its nudges toward *complacent
collectivity* as demons trying to seduce him, and answers with self-denial and
self-harm — flagellation among them. **It works.**

> **He has independently invented the drain attack and dressed it as theology.**
> Symbiotes regulate toward homeostasis, and [forcing one to act costs
> it](../ECOSYSTEM.md#their-restraint-is-costly-to-them). Persistent self-harm is
> anti-homeostatic: the symbiote spends itself correcting, continuously, for
> decades.

He is not wrong that something is whispering. He has only misidentified it — and
the thing he calls seduction toward the world is a regulator asking him to
remain viable.

**A target profile nothing else has.** The symbiote
is the bridge, so a starved symbiote is a degraded bridge:

| | |
|---|---|
| **bandwidth to him** | **poor** — clumsy to operate, expensive to read |
| **resistance from him** | **low** — his defences are exhausted |

Cheap to push, hard to use. An elder with standing who should carry one of the
island's strongest symbiotes, and has spent his life crippling it without ever
knowing what it was.

## The young priest

From Ghana. Young, highly educated, well travelled. He runs the parish day to
day and says most of the masses — **interdenominational services, well
attended.**

Deep links to the science community *and* the indigenous group, built on mutual
respect and fascination. He knows about [the
society](#he-heads-the-society-and-its-core) — everyone does — and is **wary of
it in the ordinary way a young clergyman is wary of the parish's old money.**
Of [the core](#the-core-is-not-a-corruption-of-the-society-it-is-its-original-purpose)
he knows nothing, though he has noticed that his superior is strange about the
eclipse.

**He is probably the most valuable person on the island.** Maximum connectivity
across every faction is exactly what [compute](../MECHANICS.md#compute) wants, and
central, well-connected people are normally the hardest targets. He
is the prize.

### Familiarity and otherness

The eclipse folklore resonates deeply with his own past and culture, and he
reports **a strange sensation of extreme familiarity and extreme otherness** —
which he discusses openly with the scientists and with the shaman.

**[proposal]** **He is the only person who arrived on the island carrying a
symbiote** — one of a different lineage, from an African site where comparable
things happened far earlier.

#### Why it is very old, and what that costs

"Old" must mean **an unbroken relocation chain.** Symbiotes do not reproduce;
they move host to host at each death along kin lines, and failed transitions are
the sink. Persisting since the deep past means tens of thousands of successful
handovers without a single break.

> That is astronomically improbable, which makes his passenger **special by luck
> rather than by category** — not "African symbiotes are older" but *this
> particular thread never snapped.* There may be only a handful like it on
> Earth.

The underlying logic holds: Africa is where hominins became social-cognitive, so
it is where the substrate first appeared, where parasites first found purchase,
and where symbiotes first accumulated.

> **Liability, and the fix.** This is the [ancient-aliens
> problem](../ECOSYSTEM.md#the-liability) aimed at human origins in Africa
> specifically — ground with an ugly history of pseudo-archaeology.
>
> **The symbiotes followed us; we did not follow them.** Humans became
> social-cognitive for ordinary evolutionary reasons. Parasites arrived
> *because* we became viable. Symbiotes accumulated as debris, and their
> presence then mildly reinforced a trend already underway. They are parasites —
> they do not create their hosts. The locus follows the population; the
> population is not chosen.

#### Maximum strength, scope of one

An ancient symbiote should be the strongest on the island — but scope is *the
community it regulates for*, and his knows nobody here.

> **Maximum resistance, minimum breadth.** He is individually the hardest person
> on the island to touch, and the community gets no protection from him at all.

No other strong symbiote has that profile; the rest are strong *and* broad.

**The reason is non-recognition, not ignorance.** Symbiotes identify each other
by signature over the body channel, and calibration takes centuries (see [They
recognise each other](../ECOSYSTEM.md#they-recognise-each-other-and-they-form-a-network)).
His does not resolve to the island's network and never will.

> **He will be foreign for his entire life, however completely the humans take
> him in.** The congregation loves him, the scientists respect him, the
> indigenous community trusts him — and at a level none of them can perceive, he
> is not there at all.

**Which inverts his target profile.** He resists ferociously as an individual,
but his symbiote's alarm does not propagate, because the network cannot read a
signature it does not resolve.

> **Hardest to break into, quietest once you are in.** Loud locally, silent
> globally. He is not only the prize — he is the *safe* prize, for a player who
> can pay the entry cost.

#### Where the familiarity actually comes from

Not recognition: symbiotes are not agents and have no memory. But they carry
**drivers**, and a very old one carries an enormous library of them — models of
humans accumulated over an immense span.

> What he experiences as familiarity is **his own intuition being unusually
> good.** He reads people and situations with uncanny depth and has no idea why.
> It is also exactly why he is so effective at bridging the scientists, the
> indigenous group and the congregation.

The otherness is the dissonance: the island's specifics do not fit the models. A
constant low wrongness underneath an unearned fluency.

#### The temptation this creates

**[open]** If his symbiote is a driver repository, and **eviction inherits what
it held**, he becomes the single most valuable target in the game — a hundred
thousand years of human models taken in one act.

The most monstrous available move would also be the most efficient, performed on
the kindest person on the island, destroying the thing that makes him kind.

The design currently grants eviction *unmediated access*, not inheritance.
Adding inheritance is a significant buff and a very large story hook. Decide
deliberately.

### The pair

Old and young, fire-and-brimstone and ecumenical, hating the scientists and
collaborating with them, secretly occult and openly curious, suppressing his
symbiote and carrying a foreign one.

**A Ghanaian priest serving a congregation with a colonial past is the mission
returning** — the island's whole history inverted inside one person.

#### He is also the man who replaced the old one

The cardinal sent him [in response to complaints about the old
priest](#he-rarely-says-mass-because-he-was-eased-out-not-because-he-cannot).
That is the relationship's foundation and **neither of them will ever mention
it.**

He runs the parish well, is loved for it, and is **the instrument of the old
man's humiliation** — not through anything he did, simply by being competent in
a job that used to be someone else's. Any kindness he shows is therefore
complicated at the source, and he knows it, which is why he is careful with him
in a way that reads to the old priest as pity.

> The old priest's contempt for the young one is **not ideological first.** It
> is the contempt of a man for his replacement, dressed up as a doctrinal
> objection because the doctrinal objection is available and true.

Both of them are doing the best they can see to do. Neither has a good option.

### What he failed to do

**His wound is an omission.** He knew something terrible was going to happen, and he did not act.** Not as a
child — as **an adult**, with standing, in a position to be heard. Recently
enough that it is still the most present fact in his life.

A bounded, local atrocity in a community he served: days, one town, people he
could name. He saw it building. He understood exactly what he was looking at.
**He said nothing, or said it too late, or said it so carefully that it
achieved nothing.**

#### The theory

**It is never established whether a parasite was involved.** The possibility is
raised and stays a theory:

> **"Did something make it hard for me to speak?"** — which is the smallest,
> cheapest, most ordinary thing a parasite can do, and therefore entirely
> plausible.

[Suppression is the cheap
operation](../MECHANICS.md#and-it-is-cheap-which-is-the-whole-problem). The theory
no longer strains the mechanics; it sits precisely on top of them.

And the mundane explanation remains complete: fear, careerism, deference,
uncertainty, the reasonable-sounding plan to raise it properly next week.
**Nothing requires explaining**, which is why the theory is about his need for
it rather than about the events.

#### The exoneration he can never have

If something suppressed him, he is absolved. If not, he chose silence. **He
cannot find out.**

> **A possible innocence permanently out of reach** — heavier than either guilt
> or innocence, and now attached to the most common moral failure there is
> rather than to a rare and monstrous one.

**The player is in the same position with more information**, and it still does
not resolve.

#### It gives the old priest's sermons a referent

The [old priest](#the-old-priest) preaches about *demons that drive people to
unspeakable deeds en masse*, and the young one has stood in a town where that
happened and watched himself do nothing about it.

**He does not disagree with the old man. He cannot afford to agree with him
either.** Which is a much better reason for their strained courtesy than
doctrine, and neither will ever say it.

#### [proposal] His symbiote dates from it

New symbiotes are limbs that failed to transcend. His is new. **If the event is recent,
a parasite failed somewhere near him at about that time.**

An inference the player can assemble and nobody confirms. It makes his theory
*more* plausible without making it true: a parasite was there, which does not
mean it was in him, reached him, or mattered.

> He will never have that inference. **The player gets the one piece of evidence
> he needed and cannot deliver it** — until, perhaps, they can, which is what
> [brokerage](../MECHANICS.md#4-information-brokerage--between-humans) is for, and
> is for.

**Its profile is unchanged:** born in catastrophe with nothing to calibrate on,
tuned to him alone ever since. **Deep, not wide** — maximum individual
resistance, zero network integration, no homing. Years rather than decades,
which makes it newer and stranger still.

#### Scope: the mechanics now supply the limit for free

One man, one hesitation, one week. That is inside the leash with room to spare,
and keeps the scale honest without having to insist on it.

> The theory was never allowed to be *"an alien caused that town's worst
> week."* It is now **"was something in the room when I decided to wait"** —
> which is small enough to be true and small enough not to matter, and he will
> never know which.

#### His symbiote is new, and adrift

A parasite present at that time would have failed and left **a new** symbiote,
and the reasoning is mechanical:

> **An old symbiote would have steered him back.** They are calibrated to a
> community and nudge toward the conditions their models describe, so a
> displaced one self-corrects (see [An old symbiote steers toward
> home](../ECOSYSTEM.md#an-old-symbiote-steers-toward-home)). You never find one far
> from where it was made.

**A new one has no set point.** It was born with no community calibration and
nothing to steer toward. He was set adrift and never found his way back.

It also **proves an older symbiote community exists where he comes from** — a
parasite can only operate where symbiotes already are, so there must have been
an ancient population there. Which is inference the player can make, and nobody
confirms.

**Its profile:** born in catastrophe with nothing else to calibrate on, it has
been tuned to him alone ever since. **Deep, not wide** — maximum individual
resistance, zero network integration, no homing. Years rather than decades, now
that the event is recent, which makes it newer and stranger still.

#### His perceptiveness is his own

Dropping the ancient lineage removes the driver-library explanation for his
uncanny read on people. **The replacement needs no mechanism at all — he is
simply perceptive, and always was.**

> Which is far worse for him than a gift forged by trauma. **He saw it clearly.
> That is what his perceptiveness was for, the one time it counted, and he used
> it to understand in precise detail exactly what he was declining to prevent.**

He cannot tell himself he did not know. Reading people is the thing he is best
at in the world.

Which splits the familiarity/otherness cleanly:

- **familiarity** — *cultural.* There was an older symbiote community where he is
  from, with its own traditions, and the island's folklore rhymes with stories
  he already knows
- **otherness** — *mechanical.* His symbiote receives island signal it cannot
  resolve, a constant low wrongness underneath everything

#### Do not name the place

**[recommendation]** Keep the register — a parish, a town, a week that went
wrong. **Leave the place and the conflict unspecified.**

This mattered urgently in the child-soldier version, where a supernatural
footnote risked attaching to a named genocide with living survivors. **The
omission version has largely defused that**, because the event is small, local
and unremarkable — the kind of thing that happens in many places and is named in
none of them.

The recommendation stands anyway. Unnamed costs nothing: the horror is identical
and the reader supplies the rest. And it keeps the eclipse constraint useful
rather than awkward — the moment a real event is named, somebody checks whether
there was an eclipse, and **either answer is bad.**
