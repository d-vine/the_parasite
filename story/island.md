# The island

The place. Region, the eclipse, the ground, the colonial layers, the land, what
is researchable.

↩ [STORY](../STORY.md) · [timeline](timeline.md) · [DESIGN](../DESIGN.md) · [ECOSYSTEM](../ECOSYSTEM.md) · [MECHANICS](../MECHANICS.md)


## Where

**An island.** Small enough that everyone is known to everyone, with a
**colonial past** that is not past.

The island does three things no inland setting does as well:

- **It makes the leash physical.** A spatial bound is arbitrary in a valley and
  self-evident on an island. Nobody can simply walk out.
- **It concentrates.** Everyone who comes for the eclipse is in one place, within
  the radius, which is what the [transcendence
  event](../MECHANICS.md#the-transcendence-event) needs.
- **It has a documented history of arrivals** — which is what a colonial past
  is, and what makes the folklore legible as a record.

### [proposal] The region, which is bounded; the island, which is not

> **Resolved.** The eclipse path is real and the island is not. The island is
> invented, but placed where one could plausibly stand: **north of the
> Marianas, inside the real track of the 22 July 2009 eclipse.**

**The island is invented and is not any real place.** But the islanders need a
culture, a language family, a church and a colonial history that can be
researched rather than guessed, and the eclipse is real, so the *region* is
fixed:

> **The western Pacific, beyond the northern end of the Mariana chain, on the
> 2009 centre line.**

#### Why there, and not elsewhere

| what the documents require | what it rules out |
|---|---|
| a Catholic **diocese**, two priests, a congregation | the Protestant Pacific — Samoa, Tonga, Fiji, the Cooks, most of Polynesia, **and the Marshalls** |
| conversion ~200 years before 1978, with [the old practice surviving inside it](islanders.md#the-religion-went-into-hiding-and-came-back-as-the-cult) | anywhere mission contact is 19th-century — which includes almost all of the Carolines |
| **the real 2009 track** | everything south of about 22°N. **Guam, Saipan and the whole of the Carolines saw only a partial eclipse.** The only other islands the track crossed in the Pacific were Japanese (the Tokaras, the Volcano Islands) or Marshallese atolls |
| Spain, then Germany, then Japan, then the US | Guam, which went straight from Spain to the US in 1898; the Bonins and the Volcano Islands, which were Japanese |
| [makatea geology](#the-ground) and a sacred peak | atolls, and young volcanic islands |

> **Each row rules out a lot; together they leave one small patch of ocean,
> and in reality that patch is empty.** That is the right kind of invention:
> every constraint is real, and only the island is not.

#### [proposal] Where exactly

**Around 23–24°N, 144–145°E** — about 350 km north of the last islet of the
Marianas, about 370 km east-southeast of Iwo Jima, roughly 950 km north of
Saipan.

- **Structurally it is a forearc island**: the same position as Saipan and Guam
  to the south and the Bonins to the north, between the line of volcanoes to the
  west and the trench to the east.
- **[open]** Fix the coordinates against a bathymetric chart. The trench bends
  through this stretch toward the Izu-Bonin junction, and the island has to sit
  west of it. A few tens of kilometres also decide how long totality lasts — see
  below.

### The eclipse passes overhead

From NASA's mapping table for the 2009 path, on the centre line at 145°E:

| | |
|---|---|
| **totality** | **6m38s** on the centre line, shorter the further off it the island sits (the path is 258 km wide) |
| **when** | ~02:38 UT — **about 12:38 local time** |
| **sun altitude** | **~86°.** Directly overhead; shadows all but vanish under people's feet |

- **It is the longest totality on any land that day.** The maximum, 6m39s, fell
  over open ocean a little to the north-west. The nearest real land, North Iwo
  Jima, is uninhabited, and Iwo Jima itself is a closed Japanese military
  island. **An island here is the only inhabited, reachable land anywhere near
  the maximum** — which explains [the tourists](tourists.md), and is no part of
  why Roman came.
- **July is typhoon season.** Nobody on the island can promise a clear sky, and
  everybody knows it.
- [The totality cluster](../ECOSYSTEM.md#how-symbiotes-persist-between-eclipses)
  is still invented. Only 2009 is real.


## The ground

**Volcanic underneath, dead for about thirty million years, and wearing a collar
of reef limestone lifted back out of the sea.** The form is *makatea*; the
regional model is the southern Marianas — Saipan, Guam, Rota.

Every island in this part of the ocean is volcanic underneath. What varies is
how much limestone sits on top: an atoll is a reef on a sunken volcano, and this
is a reef on one that came back up.

| when | what happened | what it left |
|---|---|---|
| ~52 million years ago | the Pacific plate begins sinking beneath the Philippine Sea plate, and the Mariana arc is born | a ceiling: **nothing on the island is older.** East of the trench is Jurassic seafloor, so the island looks out over older ground than it stands on |
| ~45–30 million years ago | an arc volcano builds up from the seabed | the interior: pillow basalts, weathered lavas, red clay, ravines, **the peak** |
| from ~30 million years ago | volcanic activity moves west; the volcano dies and sinks | — |
| ~30–5 million years ago | a reef grows on the sinking cone | hundreds of metres of limestone |
| the last few million years | uplift raises the reef back out, in steps | terraced cliffs. **The highest terrace is the oldest**, so walking uphill is walking back in time |
| the ice ages | sea level falls by up to ~120 m, and rain dissolves the exposed limestone | caves, sinkholes, karst. Seabird colonies on the cliffs; their guano reacting with the limestone makes **phosphate** |
| ~4,000 years ago | sea level settles, a metre or two above today's | the first settlers' shore lies a little inland of the modern beach |

**Not active.** The live volcanoes are the arc to the south and west — Farallon
de Pajaros, and Pagan, evacuated after its 1981 eruption — and none of them is
close enough to see.

### What the ground gives the story

- **Two halves, with two kinds of water.** The volcanic interior holds water at
  the surface: streams, gardens, taro. The limestone has none — rain drains
  straight down to a freshwater lens reached through caves and sinkholes. Guam
  is split exactly this way. Settlement, land value and sacred sites all follow
  the line between them.
- **The peak is the old volcano**, the only rock on the island that was never
  under the sea. A tradition that *the mountain was always here, and everything
  else rose out of the water* would be literally true. The telescope stands on
  the island's oldest ground.
- **The shells are in the phosphate, and the phosphate hides them.** A
  [phosphatic shell](../ECOSYSTEM.md#what-they-are-made-of) in a phosphate deposit
  looks like a nodule. And phosphate is what colonial powers came to mine on
  islands like this: a German company stripped Angaur in Palau from about 1909,
  and Japan kept going. **[proposal]** If a layer mined here, it dug through the
  shells by the tonne — and a mine's reject pile is a plausible source for the
  oldest pieces in [the
  cabinet](developers.md#he-collected-fossils-hunted-and-kept-an-amateur-naturalists-eye).


## The layers

### Before any of them

The island was settled with the rest of the Marianas, **about 3,500 years ago,
from the Philippines** — some 2,300 km of open sea, one of the first crossings
into Remote Oceania. The settlers brought no pigs, dogs or chickens; those came
with Europeans. Carolinian navigators went on sailing north to the Marianas long
after contact.

> **The islanders are the oldest arrival, and the only one that came to an empty
> island.** That distinction carries the setting's moral asymmetry, and it must
> never blur into *everyone here was a coloniser once.*

### Thin administration is why the religion survived

Spain claimed an enormous stretch of this ocean from the 1600s and **barely
administered most of it.** Missions went in; everything else was nominal.

> **That is the colonial situation that produces syncretism rather than
> extirpation.** Heavy administration destroys a practice. A mission with no
> garrison behind it gets absorbed — which is precisely how the observance
> survives two centuries inside a saints' calendar.

#### Here, the thinness has a specific cause: distance

Spain's conquest of the Marianas themselves was not thin. From 1668 it ran
through decades of war and the *reducción*: people were moved off their islands
into a handful of mission towns, and in **1698** a final expedition took the
people of the northernmost islands south. By 1700 the whole surviving
population was concentrated on Guam, Rota and Saipan, after a collapse of
roughly nine-tenths.

**This island lies about 350 km beyond the last island that expedition
cleared.** Too far for a colony with a few ships a year to empty, and too far to
garrison. A mission reached it in the early 1700s — the same years Jesuits from
Guam were trying for the Carolines, and failing: Sonsorol in 1710, then Ulithi
in 1731, where Father Cantova was speared and the mission erased within two
years. **Here, for once, it held** — as a priest with no soldiers behind him,
which is the condition that produces syncretism.

- **[proposal]** People fleeing the reducción came here. The mission that
  followed them arrived among people who had already run from missions once.

#### [proposal] What survived, and what was rebuilt

**Survival does not have to carry the whole weight.** The more plausible model —
and the one the real Marianas follow — is both at once:

| | what | how |
|---|---|---|
| **survived** | the shells, the family stories about them, the guardian spirits passed down a family | objects and household lore outlast suppressed rites. And a [carrier wants to hold a shell](../ECOSYSTEM.md#what-a-limb-does-to-its-host-it-makes-them-want-to-touch-shells) whatever the priest says: **Spain could stop the rite. It could not stop the hand** |
| **survived, in disguise** | the observance inside the saints' calendar | [the society](church.md#he-heads-the-society-and-its-core) — already written |
| **rebuilt** | the public identity: the language as a cause, the ceremony as a community event, the clans as politics, [the matriarch's](islanders.md#the-matriarch) role | a **revival**, assembled from family memory, the society's half-understood habits, and **what each colonial layer wrote down about them** |

The precedent is in the Marianas themselves. Belief in the *taotao mo'na* —
ancestral spirits who still have to be asked permission before you enter the
jungle — survived three centuries inside Catholicism, and the public CHamoru
identity has been rebuilt, deliberately and politically, since the 1970s: the
same decade the Pacific voyaging revival put a Carolinian navigator, Mau
Piailug, on *Hōkūle'a*. The other model is the Native American nations that
have rebuilt identities out of deep heritage and generations of internal exile
and persecution.

Why this is stronger than survival alone:

- **The dates line up.** A revival in full flood through the 1970s is exactly
  what [the hippies](hippies.md) walk into around 1972, and what is happening on
  [the beach](islanders.md#then-the-developer-heard-about-it-on-a-beach) in 1978.
  The teenagers handing their tradition to Roman were not leaking a secret.
  **They were part of a movement, and he was an audience** — which explains
  their pride better than survival does.
- **Every colonial layer wrote the islanders down, and a revival reads it
  back**: Spanish mission letters, German colonial ethnography, Japanese
  researchers, and in 1947–48 [CIMA](developers.md#proposal-thomas-did-a-cima-season)
  — **Thomas.** The best description of the practice before the revival may be
  an American's field notes in Chicago. That is the station's *right to
  describe* made physical, and it gives the community a stake in the archive and
  not only the scientists.
- **It sharpens the generational axis.** Who decides what is authentic — the
  elders who remember fragments, the young who are reconstructing, or the
  documents — is precisely the argument [the matriarch](islanders.md#the-matriarch)
  is in.
- **It makes the decay patterns mirror each other.** The society kept the form
  and lost the content. The community kept the content, lost the form, and is
  rebuilding it.

> **[liability]** A revival must never read as *invented*, and the accuracy of
> [the myths](islanders.md#their-myths-are-the-most-accurate-account-on-the-island)
> must not depend on its being otherwise. What survived is exactly the part that
> is a record: the shells, who held them, what came when the sun went out. What
> was rebuilt is the institution around it — which is how revivals actually
> work, and does not make them less true.

- **[open]** For the islanders thread. [church.md](church.md#which-makes-the-old-priest-and-the-matriarch-counterparts)
  currently says the community kept the ceremony **intact** and lost nothing of
  it; this proposal would revise that to *kept the content, rebuilt the
  institution.* The ceremony is only ever performed under totality, decades
  apart at best — **the hardest kind of rite to keep alive, and the most likely
  to be rebuilt from a description.**

### Four layers, not one villain

| | |
|---|---|
| **early 1700s** | Spanish mission. The conversion, and the beginning of the camouflage |
| **1899** | sold to Germany with the rest of the northern Marianas — by treaty, between two European powers, with nobody asked |
| **1914** | taken by Japan; held under League of Nations mandate. Sugar and settlers: on Saipan and Tinian, Japanese and Okinawan settlers came to outnumber the islanders many times over |
| **1944–45** | **the war.** **[proposal]** A Japanese garrison with a radar post near the bomber route from Saipan to Japan — bombed, bypassed and left to starve, like Pagan and Rota, until the surrender in September 1945. The Japanese civilians are shipped home |
| **1947** | US trusteeship: the Navy, then the Interior Department from 1951 |
| **1978–86** | self-government, then commonwealth with the US — see below |

#### The 1980s: commonwealth, and the periphery of the periphery

> **Resolved.** The island goes with the northern Marianas, and is part of them.

The real northern Marianas negotiated separately from the rest of the Trust
Territory: **a covenant in 1975**, approved by plebiscite; **self-government
under their own constitution from 1978**; **commonwealth with US citizenship**
when the trusteeship ended in 1986. **The island is inside that commonwealth** —
its own municipality and its own Senate district, not a state of its own.

| what commonwealth gave | what it cost |
|---|---|
| an elected government, a constitution, a mayor and senators of its own | sovereignty. US federal law applies wherever the covenant does not exempt |
| **Article XII**: land, and leases over 55 years, only for people of Northern Marianas descent. A deal that breaks it is **void from the start** | **land can only ever be leased** — so leases, and the people who sign them, become the island's currency |
| **control of immigration and wages.** The covenant exempted the islands from US immigration law and the federal minimum wage | that control is exactly what outside money came to buy, and Washington is taking it back [in 2009](#2009-the-gates-close) |
| US citizenship, from 1986 | the young can leave, and do |
| one non-voting delegate in Congress, first seated in January 2009 | — |

*(Free association, the route the Carolines and the Marshalls took, is the
mirror image: more sovereignty, far less outside money. Commonwealth trades
sovereignty for money and access.)*

##### The periphery of the periphery

- **The capital is Saipan, about 950 km south**: the governor, the courts, the
  hospital, the college, the permit offices. Self-determination is real, and
  most of it is exercised in Saipan by Saipan majorities. **The islanders are a
  minority inside their own commonwealth** — colonised by the empire, then
  outvoted by the colony.
- **But the Senate gives every district equal seats**, whatever its population:
  Rota and Tinian, with a few thousand people each, have as many senators as
  Saipan. So **a few thousand islanders hold 3 of 12 Senate seats** — enough to
  block legislation. That is real leverage, and it is exactly what makes them
  worth buying.
- **Access runs through Saipan or Guam**, onto a rebuilt Japanese wartime
  airstrip. Tokyo is about 1,400 km away. For the eclipse the constraint is beds
  and charter seats, which makes the hotel the gate.

Which puts a legal rupture inside the story's own timeline:

- **The hippies arrive (~1972) under the trusteeship, and the beach (1978)
  happens in the year self-government begins.**
- **Roman's land deal straddles the change, and produced a gap rather than a
  dispute.** The ground was arranged with an administering power the islanders
  had not consented to, and **the government that could have pressed [the human
  clause](station.md#the-stations-fracture) either did not exist yet or had no
  jurisdiction over the land** — see [What each layer did to the
  land](#what-each-layer-did-to-the-land). By the time it could have pressed, the
  promise was a courtesy nobody was chasing. The island got the worst of both.
- **US citizenship means the young can leave.** From 1986: a passport, a flight
  to Guam, Hawaii or the mainland, and work — or the US military, which recruits
  heavily across Micronesia. That is the legal mechanism behind [symbiotes dying
  back in atomised, mobile
  populations](../ECOSYSTEM.md#the-regulator-is-social-not-geographic). The
  organism's decline here has a *statutory* cause.

#### What each layer did to the land

**The developer could never own anything here**, and the reason is the colonial
history:

| layer | what it did to land | what was left by 1978 |
|---|---|---|
| **Spanish** | little beyond mission land | the church as one of the oldest landholders on the island |
| **German** | registration and deeds. The documented case is Pohnpei, where the 1912 land law, after the Sokehs rebellion, turned matrilineal co-ownership into **individual title held by adult men** | paper in men's names over land that custom places under the women's line |
| **Japanese** | everything surveyed and registered by 1938; **village communal land reclassified as government land** | the mountain is very likely government land from here on |
| **US** | Japanese government land inherited as **public land**. Under the Trust Territory code only Micronesian citizens could hold title. **Secretarial Order 2969 (December 1974)** returned public land to local control, **except land the military had kept** | in 1978 the return is still under way, and what the military kept is outside it |
| **commonwealth** | **Article XII** of the 1978 constitution: land and long leases only for people of Northern Marianas descent. The 1975 covenant itself leased land to the US military | the rule is constitutional, and the military's share is written into the founding deal |

So an outsider gets in only through **leases, local names on the title, and the
people who broker both** — which is a power structure, not a market.

- **[proposal] The mountain was military land.** A Japanese radar post on the
  summit; the US keeps the site after the war; it is therefore **excluded from
  the 1974 return**; and a research station funded with Navy money takes it over
  from the Navy. **The telescope stands where the radar stood**, on ground the
  islanders never got back because it was never classed as theirs to return.
- **[open]** How a military site becomes [a national park and holy
  ground](developers.md#her-father-and-what-he-left-on-the-island) at once. Most
  likely the slopes were designated later, around a summit lease that was
  carved out.
- **For [the developers thread](developers.md):** *bought an island in 1978* and
  *acquired protected coastal land* cannot be literal. His properties were in
  local names from the start, which makes [leaving them to associates, some of
  them local](developers.md#her-father-and-what-he-left-on-the-island) a
  description of what was already true — and makes the estate a live question
  in 2009, two years after his death. See [what the hole is
  made of](developers.md#proposal-what-the-hole-is-made-of).

#### [proposal] How the money came in

**Roman brought two kinds of money onto the island and tied them together.**
Navy money built the station; Japanese money was to build the resort; and **the
station's rent was what the resort's loan was secured on.**

| | when | what it paid for | what it rested on |
|---|---|---|---|
| **the land** | from 1978 | nothing — it is never sold. **Leases from the landholding families**, held through a local company with local directors and shareholders on paper: the associates | the families' signatures, bought with lease income and with [the human clause](station.md#the-stations-fracture) |
| **Navy money** | 1979–81 on | the station, through [his mother's ONR money and charter](developers.md#proposal-his-mother-is-the-other-half-of-why-the-station-exists) — and **the federal contract that let Roman build its facilities**: road, water, power, pier, a runway that takes jets | the summit is military land, leased to the station directly. **The lower campus sits on Roman's leasehold, and the station pays him rent** |
| **Japanese money** | the late-1980s bubble | the resort | **the leasehold, and an assignment of the station's rent.** The lender saw a jet runway, a power plant and a federal tenant, and lent |

> **So the US Navy was, without anyone saying so, servicing a Japanese resort
> loan.** Each money did what the other could not: federal money does not build
> resorts, and a Japanese lender does not build a runway on an island with no
> tenant.

- **Then Japan's bubble burst, in 1990–91.** The resort was never finished —
  one more reason [the great development never
  happened](developers.md#her-father-and-what-he-left-on-the-island), alongside
  the resistance. What stands is a fragment, and the debt.
- **[proposal]** The lender's man had been here before: born on the island in
  the 1930s, a settler's child, shipped to Japan in 1946. Japanese money
  returning to an island Japan held for thirty years is not neutral, and a man
  returning to where he was a child is not only an investor.
- **The 1978 leases are the weak point.** In 1978 the constitution allowed
  leases of only 40 years; the limit was later raised to 55. In *Wabol v.
  Villacrusis*, a lease signed in August 1978 for longer than the limit was
  declared **void from the start** about a decade later, and the Ninth Circuit
  upheld it. Any of Roman's first leases written the same way can be undone by
  one suit from one family — **so the families who signed them, and whoever
  they listen to, are the real holders of the resort.**

#### 2009: the gates close

The local controls that brought the money in are being taken away this year:

- **the federal minimum wage** has been stepping up every year since 2007;
- **federal immigration control begins on 28 November 2009**, four months
  after the eclipse, and the governor sued in June to stop it;
- **the economy is shrinking.** The garment industry that carried Saipan is
  gone, and Japanese tourism is falling.

> **A second public countdown, running beside the eclipse.** The elite's power
> to decide who comes and who works here ends in November, and everyone with a
> business on the island knows the date.

- **[open] Guest workers.** Under local immigration control, contract workers —
  from the Philippines, China and elsewhere — build things and staff the hotel,
  on permits their employers hold. From November federal rules apply, and
  nobody knows what that means for them yet. The documents have no such
  faction, but [the brother's event](developers.md#who-is-supposed-to-be-there)
  already lists *immigration status* among its levers, and that lever only
  works on them. **[liability]** If they come in, they are people with their
  own stakes, not a supply of victims.
- **For the hippies thread:** [their legal
  status](hippies.md#they-walked-in-legally-which-is-worse) was written against
  independence in the mid-1980s. Under commonwealth they are US citizens
  throughout, and Article XII is what changes for them: they can never own the
  ground they live on.

### What to research, since it is now researchable

CHamoru and Carolinian (Refaluwasch) kinship and clan structure; matrilineal
land tenure and how it collided with registered title; the Spanish conquest of
the Marianas and the reducción; the Jesuit attempts on the Carolines; the German
and Japanese administrations; the bypassed islands of the Pacific War; Trust
Territory land law and the Northern Marianas covenant; Catholic syncretic
practice in the region, including the *taotao mo'na*; the Pacific cultural
revival of the 1970s.

> **Name the influences, invent the specifics.** Vagueness is not respect — it
> produces generic islanders. A named region with invented particulars is both
> more honest and more writable than an unplaceable one.
>
> And **do not map the island onto a living community.** The composite is the
> point: it is why this can be researched properly without being *about* anyone.
> **Now that the island sits at the end of the Marianas, the pull toward CHamoru
> people specifically is stronger** — keep the Carolinian strand, and keep the
> specifics invented.
